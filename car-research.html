Car Buying Guide

Find the right car for your life.

Don't get overwhelmed by hundreds of models. Understand the engines, the common faults, and match your lifestyle to the perfect type of car.

Petrol: The UK Default

Petrol engines are smoother, quieter, and generally cheaper to buy and maintain than diesels. They are perfect for city driving and low annual mileage (under 12,000 miles a year). Modern small petrol engines (like the Ford EcoBoost or VW TSI) use turbos to give great fuel economy and power.

Pros

  • Cheaper to buy and service
  • Quieter and smoother to drive
  • No DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) issues
  • Immediate power delivery

Cons & Common Issues

  • Higher fuel consumption on motorways
  • Timing Belts: Many small modern petrols (like Ford EcoBoost or Vauxhall Ecotec) use a "wet belt" which requires expensive replacement every 6-10 years.
  • Carbon Build-Up: Direct Injection (GDI) engines can suffer from carbon build-up on intake valves.

Diesel: The Motorway Cruiser

Diesel engines offer incredible torque and high fuel economy. They are the kings of the motorway. However, they are absolutely the wrong choice for someone who only does short trips to the shops, as modern emission controls will clog up.

Pros

  • Excellent fuel economy on long journeys
  • High torque (great for towing or heavy loads)
  • Engines often last 200,000+ miles if maintained

Cons & Common Issues

  • DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter): The #1 issue. If you only do short trips, the filter cannot regenerate and will block, costing £1,000+.
  • EGR Valve: Exhaust Gas Recirculation valves get coked up with soot.
  • Swirl Flaps: Common on older BMW and some other diesels; can cause catastrophic engine failure if they break off.

Hybrid: The City King

A hybrid (like a Toyota Yaris or Corolla) combines a small petrol engine with an electric battery. You don't plug it in; the car charges the battery itself using braking energy. They are incredibly reliable and brilliant in stop-start city traffic.

Pros

  • Incredible reliability (Toyota/Lexus are legendary)
  • Superb fuel economy in towns and cities
  • Automatic gearbox as standard (usually e-CVT)
  • No charging cables required

Cons & Common Issues

  • Engine can sound "drony" under hard acceleration
  • Not as efficient on the motorway as a diesel
  • Battery Inverter Cooling: Ensure the hybrid battery cooling fan (usually located near the rear seats) is clean and not blocked by dust or pet hair.

Electric (EV): The Future

Fully electric cars offer silent, instant acceleration and incredibly low running costs. However, you must have a way to charge at home, and long journeys require planning around charging stops.

Pros

  • Lowest running costs (if charging at home)
  • Zero road tax (currently) and no ULEZ charges
  • Instant, blistering acceleration
  • Almost zero maintenance (no oil, no belts)

Cons & Common Issues

  • High purchase price
  • Battery Degradation: Older EVs lose some range over time. Always check the State of Health (SoH) of the battery before buying.
  • Public rapid chargers can be expensive and unreliable.

Common Issues by Make

Ford (Fiesta, Focus, Puma)

▼
The 1.0 EcoBoost Engine: This is a brilliant engine, but it uses a "wet belt" (a timing belt submerged in oil). If this belt snaps, it destroys the engine. It MUST be changed every 10 years or 100,000 miles (check for proof of this!). The oil pump belt is also a known weak point.

Other checks: Look for leaks from the thermostat housing and check the rear wheel arches for rust on older Fiestas.

Vauxhall (Corsa, Astra, Mokka)

▼
1.2 PureTech / Ecotec Engines: Similar to Ford, many modern Vauxhall petrol engines use a wet timing belt. These can degrade and clog the oil pickup pipe, leading to catastrophic engine failure. Ensure the belt has been replaced or budget for it immediately.

Other checks: Check that the air conditioning blows cold (condensers are a common failure), and listen for suspension knocks over bumps.

Volkswagen (Golf, Polo, T-Roc)

▼
DSG Gearboxes: VW's automatic DSG gearboxes are fast and smooth, but the older "dry clutch" versions (usually on 1.4/1.5 petrols) can suffer from juddering. Ensure the gearbox oil has been changed if it has a wet clutch.

Other checks: Check for a leaky water pump (very common), and listen for a rattling timing chain on cold start (especially on older 1.2 or 1.4 TSI engines).
