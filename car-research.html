The CarExplain Guide

Understand cars. Know your costs. Never get ripped off.

We break down the intimidating world of car buying and garage repairs into plain English. Understand the engines, the common faults, and exactly what you should be paying.

Petrol: The UK Default

Petrol engines are smoother, quieter, and generally cheaper to maintain than diesels. They are perfect for city driving and low annual mileage (under 12,000 miles a year). Modern small petrol engines (like the Ford EcoBoost or VW TSI) use turbos to give great fuel economy and power.

The Good

  • Cheaper to buy and service
  • Quieter and smoother to drive
  • No DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter) issues
  • Immediate power delivery

The Bad (Common Faults)

  • Wet Belts: Many modern petrols (Ford 1.0 EcoBoost, Vauxhall 1.2) use a timing belt submerged in oil. These degrade and can destroy the engine. Replacing them costs £500-£800.
  • Carbon Build-Up: Direct Injection engines can get carbon build-up on the valves.
  • Coil Packs: Spark plug coil packs are a common failure, causing misfires.

Diesel: The Motorway Cruiser

Diesel engines offer incredible torque and high fuel economy. They are the kings of the motorway. However, they are absolutely the wrong choice for someone who only does short trips to the shops, as modern emission controls will clog up.

The Good

  • Excellent fuel economy on long journeys
  • High torque (great for towing or heavy loads)
  • Engines often last 200,000+ miles if maintained

The Bad (Common Faults)

  • DPF (Diesel Particulate Filter): The #1 issue. If you only do short trips, the filter cannot regenerate and will block. Replacing one costs £1,000 - £2,000.
  • EGR Valve: Exhaust Gas Recirculation valves get coked up with soot, causing rough idling.
  • Dual Mass Flywheel (DMF): Diesels have huge torque which wears out the DMF, causing violent vibrations. A clutch + DMF replacement is £800 - £1,500.

Hybrid: The City King

A hybrid (like a Toyota Yaris or Corolla) combines a small petrol engine with an electric battery. You don't plug it in; the car charges the battery itself using braking energy. They are incredibly reliable and brilliant in stop-start city traffic.

The Good

  • Incredible reliability (Toyota/Lexus are legendary)
  • Superb fuel economy in towns and cities
  • Automatic gearbox as standard (usually e-CVT)
  • No charging cables required

The Bad (Common Faults)

  • Engine can sound "drony" under hard acceleration
  • Not as efficient on the motorway as a diesel
  • Battery Cooling: Ensure the hybrid battery cooling fan is clean and not blocked by dust or pet hair.

Electric (EV): The Future

Fully electric cars offer silent, instant acceleration and incredibly low running costs. However, you must have a way to charge at home, and long journeys require planning around charging stops.

The Good

  • Lowest running costs (if charging at home)
  • Zero road tax (currently) and no ULEZ charges
  • Almost zero maintenance (no oil, no belts)

The Bad (Common Faults)

  • High purchase price
  • Battery Degradation: Older EVs lose some range. Always check the State of Health (SoH).
  • Public rapid chargers can be expensive and unreliable.

What to look out for by Make

Every brand has a skeleton in its closet. Here are the most critical issues to check before buying.

Ford (Fiesta, Focus, Puma)

▼
The 1.0 EcoBoost Engine: This is a brilliant engine, but it uses a "wet belt" (a timing belt submerged in oil). If this belt snaps, it destroys the engine. It MUST be changed every 10 years or 100,000 miles. The oil pump belt is also a known weak point.
⚠️ Warning: If buying a used Ford, ask for written proof that the wet belt has been changed. If they can't prove it, walk away or budget £700 for immediate replacement.
Other checks: Look for leaks from the thermostat housing and check the rear wheel arches for rust on older Fiestas.

Vauxhall (Corsa, Astra, Mokka)

▼
1.2 PureTech / Ecotec Engines: Similar to Ford, many modern Vauxhall petrol engines use a wet timing belt. These can degrade and clog the oil pickup pipe, leading to catastrophic engine failure.
⚠️ Warning: These engines are notorious for oil consumption. Check the oil level immediately and look for blue smoke from the exhaust.
Other checks: Check that the air conditioning blows cold (condensers are a common failure), and listen for suspension knocks over bumps.

Volkswagen / Audi / Skoda / Seat (VAG Group)

▼
DSG Gearboxes: VW's automatic DSG gearboxes are fast and smooth, but the older "dry clutch" versions (usually on 1.4/1.5 petrols) can suffer from juddering. The "wet clutch" versions (on diesels and higher-power petrols) require an oil change every 40,000 miles. Water Pumps: A leaky water pump is a rite of passage for VW owners. Listen for a high-pitched squeal or check for pink crusty residue around the engine bay. Timing Chains: Older 1.2 and 1.4 TSI engines can suffer from timing chain tensioner failure, causing a rattling noise on cold start.

BMW (1 Series, 3 Series, X1)

▼
The N47 Diesel Engine: This engine (used in many 2007-2015 models) is famous for timing chain failure. The chain is located at the back of the engine, meaning the gearbox and engine often have to be removed to fix it (a £2,000+ bill).
⚠️ Warning: Listen for a whining, supercharger-like noise from the rear of the engine on cold start. Avoid high-mileage N47 diesels unless the chain has been done.
Other checks: Check the PCV valve (crankcase breather) and look for oil leaks around the rocker cover.

The Garage Guide: Costs & Regions

As a woman, walking into a garage can sometimes feel intimidating. You might worry about being overcharged or talked into repairs you don't need. Knowledge is power. Here is a realistic guide to what common repairs cost in the UK, and why the price varies depending on where you live.

Common Repair Costs (Parts & Labour)

Front Brake Pads & Discs£150 - £250
Full Service (Oil & Filters)£180 - £300
Clutch Replacement (Manual)£400 - £800
Timing Belt / Wet Belt£500 - £800
Alternator Replacement£350 - £550
DPF Cleaning / Replacement£300 - £1,500

Why do prices vary by region?

Labour rates are the biggest factor. A garage in London charges much more per hour than a garage in Yorkshire.

London & South East£100 - £150 / hr
South West & Midlands£70 - £100 / hr
North of England & Wales£50 - £80 / hr
Independent vs Main DealerDealers charge up to 50% more

How to avoid being ripped off:

  • Ask for the old parts: If they replace your brake pads, ask to see the old ones. It proves they actually did the work.
  • Ask for a breakdown: A quote should separate the cost of the parts from the labour hours. If they just give you one big number, ask them to break it down.
  • Don't be afraid to get a second quote: If a repair sounds expensive, politely say, "Thank you, I need to think about it." A good garage will respect this. A dodgy garage will pressure you.
  • Use the CarExplain AI: Before agreeing to a repair, run the symptom through our AI tool. If the AI says it's likely a £150 sensor, and the garage says you need a £1,000 engine rebuild, you know to walk away.
